## Why OpenAI killed Sora

The economics were broken. Sora was burning through an estimated $8–12 million per month in compute costs while generating less than $2 million in subscription revenue. Video generation requires dramatically more compute than text or images, and Sora never reached the user volume to close that gap.

Engagement fell sharply after the initial press coverage. Casual users hit the 12-second clip limit, found the $200/month Pro plan unworkable for occasional use, and left. The professional video creators who could justify that monthly spend found quality too inconsistent for client work. Sora was caught between a consumer audience it couldn't retain and a professional market it wasn't quite ready for.

OpenAI gave users one month's notice to export their work. All Sora account data was permanently deleted after the April 26 web shutdown.

## The API stays until September — what this means

The `sora-2` and `sora-2-pro` API endpoints remain active until September 24, 2026. If you have production pipelines running on Sora's API, that's enough time to evaluate replacements properly.

The consumer app, sora.com, and the ChatGPT interface for video generation are all gone. The API-only window is specifically for developers migrating automated workflows.

## What to use instead

The broader AI video category is more capable than Sora's failure might suggest. Three tools stand out as genuine replacements.

---

### Google Veo 3.1 — best overall replacement

Veo 3.1 is the most capable Sora replacement for most use cases. The differentiating feature is native synchronized audio — dialogue, sound effects, and ambient sound generated in one pass, without a separate audio step. No other mainstream video model offered this before Veo, and it remains a real edge over the field.

Output goes up to 4K (native 1080p with high-quality upscaling). Spatial audio generates three-dimensional sound environments that track with the video content.

Access tiers:
- **Free:** limited generation via Google AI Studio
- **AI Pro ($19.99/month):** Veo 3.1 access through Gemini, moderate generation limits
- **AI Ultra ($249.99/month):** higher limits, priority access, full feature set
- **API:** $0.03–$0.60 per second depending on quality tier

For users who were on ChatGPT Plus at $20/month primarily for Sora access, AI Pro at $19.99 is the closest direct replacement at the same price point — and the audio generation is a genuine capability upgrade.

---

### Kling AI 3.0 — best for value

Kling 3.0, from Chinese tech company Kuaishou, launched February 5, 2026. At Standard pricing of $6.99/month, it currently offers the best capability-per-dollar in AI video.

Specific advantages over Sora: clips up to two minutes (Sora maxed at 12 seconds), 4K output, native multilingual audio, and the most reliable text rendering of any video model available. Signs, logos, and price tags stay legible in Kling-generated video — a practical differentiator for anything with brand elements in frame.

Plans: Free, Standard ($6.99/month), Pro ($25.99/month), Premier ($64.99/month). One catch: failed generations consume credits with no automatic refund, and credits don't roll over between billing cycles. Budget for iteration when exploring new concepts.

---

### Runway Gen-4.5 — best for cinematic work

If your Sora use case was cinematic quality — controlled camera moves, consistent character motion across cuts, specific shot composition — Runway Gen-4.5 is the closest replacement. The creative control tools (motion brushes, camera direction, reference-image-to-video) are the most mature in the category, and Gen-4.5 specifically addressed the character consistency across cuts that made earlier Runway versions difficult for narrative work.

We covered the full tool set in our [Runway AI review](/runway-ai-2026-review/). Runway's Standard plan starts at $15/month — half what ChatGPT Plus cost for Sora access, with more production-grade controls.

### Others worth knowing

**Seedance 2.0** (ByteDance) accepts up to 12 inputs — images, video clips, and audio files — for multi-modal reference generation. Useful for remixing existing footage into new contexts.

**Luma Ray 3** positions itself as a "reasoning video model" that interprets creative intent before generating. The 16-bit HDR output is the highest colour depth available in AI video, relevant if your workflow ends in professional post-production.

## What Sora's shutdown actually means for AI video

Sora failing on unit economics doesn't mean the video generation category is struggling. The tools that replaced it — Veo, Kling, Runway — got to competitiveness partly because they're built more efficiently for serving video at scale.

The meaningful gap from what Sora promised still exists: consistent characters across longer sequences, production-reliable face rendering, and controlled narrative across more than 12 seconds. Those remain hard problems. The current alternatives are genuinely better than Sora was, but the category as a whole hasn't solved the hard parts yet.
